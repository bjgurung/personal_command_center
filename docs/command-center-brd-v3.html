02 — privacy & data sovereignty

Privacy & Data Sovereignty

Privacy by minimization: collect only what is required for reporting. The system never requests or stores bank login credentials, full account or card numbers, SSNs, EINs/TINs, or identity documents. Data is processed in infrastructure accounts controlled by the owner; hosting providers necessarily process stored and transmitted data.

What apps like Empower/Monarch do
What this system does instead
Plaid connects to your bank, reads all transactions, stores them on their servers
You export CSV from your bank's own site, upload to your own Supabase instance
Your transaction history is a product — they analyze, aggregate, and monetize it
Your data sits in a Postgres database only you can access (RLS + 2FA)
Bank credentials stored by a third-party middleware company
No bank credentials stored anywhere — CSV is a read-only export
Data deletion requests are "processed" — unclear if fully honored
Owner-controlled database, complete export, deletion workflow, and independent encrypted backup
Subject to their privacy policy changes, data breaches, acquisitions
Owner chooses providers and can export or migrate the data
Data flow principle: Financial data enters through manual entry or a user-exported CSV. No bank APIs, OAuth connections, or aggregation middleware are used. Before import, account-number columns are ignored; the original CSV is parsed client-side and discarded after confirmation. Only normalized transaction fields are retained.

03 — scope

What Goes in the Command Center

Ten modules across two domains: personal financial health and consulting operations. The personal finance layer tells you where your money goes. The business layer tells you where it comes from. The analytics layer tells you what to do about it. Together, they answer the question no single tool can: "Am I actually getting ahead?"

🏠

Personal Finance

Your full personal burn rate — mortgage, groceries, utilities, everything. This is what makes the "runway" metric real.

  • Monthly recurring bills: mortgage/rent, car payment, insurance (auto/home/life), utilities, phone, internet, subscriptions
  • Variable spending: groceries, dining out, gas/transportation, clothing, entertainment, personal care
  • Periodic expenses: property tax, HOA, vehicle registration, medical copays, home maintenance
  • Savings & investments: 401k contributions, IRA, brokerage transfers, emergency fund
  • Debt tracking: credit card balances, student loans, auto loans — minimum payments and payoff targets
  • Monthly personal burn rate (total fixed + average variable)
  • Spending by category with month-over-month trend
  • 50/30/20 rule tracker: needs vs. wants vs. savings as % of take-home
  • Net worth snapshot: assets − liabilities (updated monthly)
💰

Financial Core (Business)

Unified P&L across all income streams + personal expenses — the one view that doesn't exist anywhere.

  • W-2 income tracking (manual paystub entry, net and gross, YTD running total)
  • Consulting revenue per client (auto-populated from paid invoices)
  • Business expense tracking with categories and receipt photo upload
  • Combined cash flow waterfall: total income (W-2 + consulting) vs. total outflow (personal + business) vs. net
  • Rolling 12-month trend line across all income and expenses
  • Configurable tax reserve planner, clearly labeled as an estimate
  • Tax set-aside tracker (recommended vs. actual saved)
  • Infrastructure cost monitor per client project
  • True financial runway: liquid savings ÷ monthly personal burn rate
  • Year-end summary exportable as PDF
📋

Business Records — Secondary Page

Supporting records for invoices and consulting income; never shown as detailed client cards on the homepage.

  • Client records: status (prospect / active / paused / completed), contract value, start date
  • Proposal pipeline: name, client, estimated value, date sent, follow-up dates
  • Revenue, direct expenses, outstanding invoices, and cash margin per client
  • Deliverable tracker per engagement with completion %
  • Engagement health score (payment timeliness, scope creep, communication frequency)
  • Client communication log (last contact, next action)
🧾

Invoice Engine

Simple invoicing built for 3–10 clients, not enterprise accounting.

  • Create invoices using fixed-fee, milestone, retainer, or manually entered service line items
  • Invoice status lifecycle: draft → sent → paid → overdue
  • Aging alerts at 15 / 30 / 60 days overdue
  • PDF generation with your branding (Supabase Edge Function)
  • Invoice history and search
  • Annual 1099-ready summary per client
🖥️

Infrastructure Monitor — Optional

A separate operational utility, excluded from the financial dashboard and MVP.

  • Supabase project usage per client: database size, storage, egress, edge function invocations
  • Cloudflare Pages/Workers metrics: bandwidth, requests, deploy status
  • Free-tier headroom gauge (% consumed with threshold at 80%)
  • Cost projection: "at current growth, you'll hit paid tier in X weeks"
  • Uptime ping for deployed client sites (Edge Function cron)
📈

Goals & Growth

The "where am I going" layer — not just where things are.

  • Quarterly revenue targets vs. actual with gap analysis
  • Client acquisition funnel (leads → proposals → wins, conversion rate)
  • SaaS productization tracker (Revenue Intelligence Engine milestones)
  • Skill / certification tracking
  • MRR tracker (when SaaS revenue starts)
🔔

Notifications & Digest

Don't wait until you open the dashboard — the dashboard comes to you.

  • In-app notification center (bell icon, unread count)
  • Weekly email digest every Monday 7 AM (Supabase Edge Function + Resend free tier)
  • Alert triggers: overdue invoice, free-tier threshold, spending anomaly, tax reserve gap
  • Digest includes: income, spending, overdue invoices, upcoming bills, savings, and tax reserve status
📊

Weekly Scorecard

Seven numbers that summarize financial health. Glanceable on mobile, with no infrastructure status or detailed client information.

  • Net cash position (liquid savings − upcoming obligations)
  • Personal spend this week vs. weekly budget
  • Financial runway in months
  • Total revenue recognized this week
  • Income sources: W-2, consulting, and other income
  • Outstanding AR (unpaid invoices total)
  • Savings and tax reserve progress
  • Trend arrows vs. prior week on all metrics
🧠

Analytics & Recommendations

The intelligence layer. Analyzes your data and tells you what to do — not just what happened. Rules-based, no AI, runs in Postgres.

  • Financial analysis: spending trends by category, income vs. expense ratio, cash flow forecast (3 months), burn rate trajectory, tax optimization gaps
  • Business analysis: client cash margin, direct-cost trends, revenue concentration risk, invoice aging, and pipeline conversion
  • Predictive: runway projection, infra cost projection, tax-reserve shortfall, and cash-flow pressure
  • Recommendations: actionable cards such as "increase the tax reserve by $400," "Curry Gate direct costs rose 18%," or "70% revenue concentration risk"
  • Delivery: insight cards on dashboard, weekly digest, in-app notifications for urgent triggers

07 — recommendations

Recommendations

Architecture recommendations

Use Supabase Edge Functions for all server-side logic

Don't split logic between Cloudflare Workers and Supabase Edge Functions. Keep Workers as a dumb CDN/proxy layer only. All business logic — cron jobs, PDF generation, email sending, API integrations — runs in Edge Functions. One runtime, one deploy target, one set of secrets in Supabase Vault. Simpler debugging, simpler architecture.

Start with manual entry, automate later

Build CSV import in Phase 1 but don't over-invest in parsing. For 3 clients and a W-2, you'll have ~20 transactions/month. Manual entry with category dropdowns is faster than debugging CSV column mapping across 4 different bank export formats. Automate when friction becomes real, not theoretical.

Build a configurable tax reserve planner

Use tax-year parameters and user-entered filing assumptions, W-2 wages, withholding, and net consulting income. Account for the Social Security wage base and applicable Medicare rules. Label every result as an estimate and review it annually against the current IRS Form 1040-ES.

Use Supabase Realtime for the dashboard, not polling

Subscribe only where live synchronization materially helps, such as invoices and notifications. Realtime is optional rather than an MVP dependency.

Data & financial recommendations

Separate "revenue recognized" from "cash received"

When an invoice is marked "paid," record both: (1) income_records entry with the payment date and (2) update the invoice status. Your P&L shows revenue by invoice period; your cash flow shows money by payment date. These are different views of the same event, and conflating them will make your tax summary wrong.

Track the "true cost" of each client

Allow direct expenses to be tagged to a client. Report payments received minus direct costs as cash margin, with drill-down to underlying records; do not label it accounting profit.

Weekly scorecard as the default landing page

Make the weekly scorecard the homepage: usable cash, upcoming obligations, current spending, income sources, outstanding receivables, runway, and savings/tax reserve progress. Do not show infrastructure health or individual client cards. Every metric must show freshness and open the relevant supporting view.

Use one canonical transaction layer

Normalize every imported or manually entered financial event before reporting. Preserve source, import batch, original description, account label, currency, reconciliation state, and duplicate fingerprint.

UX & product recommendations

Mobile-first capture, desktop-first analysis

Expense capture and invoice status changes must be one-handed on mobile. Detailed reconciliation, imports, forecasting, and analysis should use the full desktop viewport.

Build a "quick capture" floating action button

On mobile, provide two actions from anywhere: Add Expense and Create Invoice.

Use the weekly email digest as your accountability system

The Monday digest should lead with exceptions: overdue invoices, unusual spending, low runway, tax-reserve gaps, upcoming obligations, and infrastructure warnings. Keep detailed transactions and sensitive balances out of email by default.

Add a "Financial Runway" metric — now powered by real personal data

Calculate: liquid savings ÷ monthly personal burn rate = months of runway if all consulting revenue stopped tomorrow. Because you're now tracking actual mortgage, groceries, insurance, and every other personal expense, this number is real — not a guess. Display it on the scorecard. It's the number that tells you whether you can afford to drop a bad client or invest time in SaaS productization.

Personal finance recommendations

Track W-2 net AND gross — they serve different purposes

Gross salary informs tax-reserve planning while net take-home drives cash flow and personal budgeting. Store both with their source period and never collect SSNs, employer tax identifiers, or full paystub documents.

Use a "personal burn rate" not a "budget"

Budgets are aspirational and break on the first unexpected expense. Your burn rate is a backward-looking fact: what you actually spent over the last 3 months, averaged. Use it for the runway calculation, for deciding whether to take on a risky client, and for knowing when a raise is mandatory vs. nice-to-have. Display both the fixed component (mortgage, car, insurance — predictable) and the variable component (groceries, dining, gas — controllable).

Set up recurring bills with due dates and auto-pay flags

The recurring_bills table isn't just for tracking — it's for projecting. If you know your mortgage is $X on the 1st, car payment is $Y on the 15th, and insurance is $Z on the 20th, the dashboard can show you "between now and the end of the month, you have $W in fixed obligations left." That's a different (and more useful) number than your bank balance.

Don't over-categorize personal spending

You need ~10 personal categories, not 50. Groceries, dining out, gas, clothing, entertainment, medical, personal care, home maintenance, gifts, miscellaneous. That's it. The 50/30/20 split (needs/wants/savings) is more actionable than tracking whether you spent $4.50 on a latte vs. a smoothie. Assign each category a budget_type (need/want) at the category level, not per transaction.

What I'd add beyond the original scope

Contract & document vault

Store MSAs, SOWs, NDAs, and W-9s in Supabase Storage with metadata (client, type, expiration date). Alert 30 days before a contract expires. At tax time, every 1099-relevant document is in one place. Not a full document management system — just an indexed file drawer.

Retrospective log

After each engagement, record what went well, what did not, delivery outcome, cash margin, and whether you would work with the client again.

Contract-value history

Track contract-value changes, direct costs, revisions, and delivery outcomes over time. This supports pricing reviews without collecting hour data.

Annual business review generator

At year-end, generate a one-page summary of income, expenses, cash surplus, top client, outstanding receivables, clients acquired/lost, and goal attainment. Clearly distinguish management reporting from formal tax or accounting statements.

08 — data architecture

Data Architecture & Schema

Canonical transactions form the ingestion layer, with personal and business reporting views preserving domain separation. Clients and projects are business dimensions. All user-owned records include user_id for RLS, created_at, updated_at, and deleted_at; critical changes also write immutable audit events.

Key design choice — income: income_records is the single source of truth for all revenue. W-2 income is entered manually (monthly paystub — both net take-home and gross). Consulting income is auto-created when an invoice status changes to "paid." The P&L view queries income_records by source type — it never queries invoices directly for revenue figures.
Key design choice — personal vs. business expenses: Personal and business expenses live in separate tables (personal_expenses vs. expenses). They are never commingled. The IRS requires clear separation for Schedule C deductions — mixing them in one table with a flag is a tax audit risk. The combined cash flow waterfall queries both tables and stacks the bars, but the underlying data stays cleanly separated. Recurring personal bills (mortgage, utilities, subscriptions) live in recurring_bills with a frequency and due-day so the dashboard can project future months. Debts and assets live in debts_and_assets for the net worth snapshot — balances are updated monthly.
09 — system architecture

System Architecture

Single-user SPA backed by Supabase. Edge Functions handle suitable server-side logic. The design targets current free tiers, but quotas and product terms must be verified before deployment and monitored over time.

Cost target: use open-source components and current free tiers where practical. The design does not guarantee permanent $0 operation; provider quotas, backup storage, domains, and email delivery may introduce costs.
10 — automation engine

Automation Engine

The goal is to reduce weekly manual effort to approximately 5–10 minutes after categorization rules mature. Client-side parsing minimizes uploaded source data, while configured hosting and email providers still process the data sent through their services.

CSV smart import (Phase 1)

IDAutomationHow it works
AU-01Multi-bank CSV parserAuto-detect bank format (Chase, BofA, Wells Fargo, credit unions) by header pattern matching. Map columns to: date, description, amount, category. Store parser profiles so each bank is recognized on second upload.
AU-02Auto-routing: personal vs. businessKeyword-based router. "MORTGAGE", "KROGER", "SHELL" → personal_expenses. "GITHUB", "CLOUDFLARE", "SUPABASE" → business expenses. New vendors prompt once, then remember forever via a vendor_rules table.
AU-03Smart categorizationEach transaction description is matched against a category_rules table: vendor keyword → category + budget_type (need/want). Rules are seeded with ~50 common patterns and learn from every manual correction you make. After 2 months, 90%+ of transactions should auto-categorize correctly.
AU-04Duplicate detectionHash each transaction (date + amount + vendor) to prevent double-import when you upload overlapping CSV date ranges. Flag near-duplicates (same amount, ±1 day) for manual review instead of silently dropping them.
AU-05Batch uploadUpload multiple CSVs at once (checking, savings, credit card). Parser identifies which bank each file came from and routes accordingly. One drag-and-drop, all accounts processed.

AU-06Auto-generate expected expensespg_cron runs on the 1st of each month. For each recurring_bill, generate a personal_expenses record with status "expected." When the matching CSV transaction arrives, status flips to "confirmed" and any amount difference is flagged.

Automation maturity timeline

Week 1: Everything is manual. You type expenses, enter income, create invoices by hand. ~30 min/week.
Week 4: CSV smart import is working. Recurring bills auto-generate. Tax and burn rate recalculate automatically. ~15 min/week (one CSV upload + review).
Week 8: Smart categorization has learned recurring vendors. Invoice templates and exception notifications reduce repetitive work. ~5–10 min/week for import and review.
Week 12: Weekly digest and monthly reports focus on finances and exceptions. Optional operational monitoring remains separate. ~5–10 min/week steady state.
